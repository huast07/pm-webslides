PM demo: backtest, DCF and income flow

Moving averages, a DCF and an income flow

Simulated data; illustrative; not investment advice; not affiliated with Alphabet.

The idea: follow the trend with two moving averages

  • Hold the stock when the fast average is above the slow one; hold cash otherwise.

  • A fast window (20 days) reacts quickly; a slow window (100 days) shows the trend.

  • Each switch between stock and cash pays a cost per trade.

  • We judge it against simply buying and holding the stock.

Note 1
  1. Note 1

    How it's worked out: the fast average is the mean of the last 20 closes and the slow average the mean of the last 100. The signal is 1 (in the stock) when fast > slow, else 0 (cash). The strategy earns the next day's return times yesterday's signal, less the cost on each day the signal changes.

Sources and method

Prices are a random walk: each trading day the price moves by a normally distributed amount with a drift of 7% a year and swings of 20% a year, from a start of 100. New market draws another path. Simulated data; illustrative; not investment advice.

The strategy holds the market while the fast average is above the slow one and holds cash otherwise. It acts the day after a cross, and each trade costs the cost per trade. Growth a year, swings a year, the Sharpe ratio (no risk-free rate), the worst fall and the number of trades are worked out on the Backtest sheet. The heatmap reruns the whole backtest for every pair of windows.

Illustrative inputs, not a real company: revenue of $1,000m growing 8% a year, a 30% EBITDA margin, 21% tax and capex of 5% of revenue. Free cash flow is discounted at the WACC; the terminal value grows the last forecast year by the terminal growth rate (Gordon growth) and is discounted with it.
